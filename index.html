BioSynth Theoretical Manual

1. Lipinski's Rule of 5

Formulated by Christopher A. Lipinski in 1997, this rule predicts if a chemical compound has chemical and physical properties that would make it a likely orally active drug in humans. A drug candidate should have:

  • Molecular Weight ≤ 500 Daltons
  • Partition Coefficient (LogP) ≤ 5.0 (estimates lipophilicity)
  • Hydrogen Bond Donors ≤ 5 (sum of NH and OH groups)
  • Hydrogen Bond Acceptors ≤ 10 (sum of N and O atoms)

2. Molecular Docking Dynamics

Docking is a computational method that predicts the preferred orientation of one molecule (ligand) to a second (receptor/protein) when bound to each other. The binding affinity is represented as Gibbs Free Energy (ΔG):

  • ΔG < -6.0 kcal/mol: Good drug binding
  • ΔG < -8.0 kcal/mol: Exceptional binding affinity (tight lock-and-key fit)
  • Inhibitory Constant (Ki): Lower Ki values indicate a more potent drug, needing less concentration to inhibit target functions.
